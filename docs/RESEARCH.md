# Research notes: annotated bibliography

Status: historical design-time bibliography and dated observations, not a current implementation inventory. At the original survey checkpoint the library did not yet exist; current installed unstable SDK and integrated typed C++ proof are recorded in [POC_PLAN current provenance](POC_PLAN.md#current-typed-c-cutover-provenance) and [README](../README.md#install-and-use-the-unstable-c-sdk). Every "decision informed" entry remains a design inference, not an empirical result of the cited work; later implementation does not retroactively turn those proposals into observed results.

Lessons note (honest correction): the first bibliography covered papers, specifications and third-party projects but no primary vendor documentation for the reasoning-state rules. Because of that gap an earlier draft of the design treated "do signatures interoperate across hosts" as unverified, when the vendor's own thinking documentation already states part of the answer (section 8). The premise was wrong, and the origin design (decision D4) was reworked once the pages were read. Vendor documentation is now a first-class source in section 8, and any claim about vendor behaviour in the design or conformance plan must cite either a vendor page or a live observation (section 9), or be labelled `[INFERENCE]`.

## How to read the evidence tags

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[read full text]` | The body text was opened. Where the survey recorded which sections were read, they are named. It does not mean the whole paper or appendices were read. |
| `[read abstract]` | Only the full abstract and metadata were read. |
| `[summary only]` | Only a search snippet or a second-hand description was seen. No claim rests on it alone. |
| `[read source]` | (Projects) Source code, issues and pull requests were read in a shallow clone at a pinned commit. Nothing was built or executed, and live behaviour was not observed. |
| `[read docs]` | Official documentation or a specification page was read. The implementation was not. |

Notes on the tags:

- Levels are copied from the literature and survey reports. None was upgraded.
- Where two reports read the same item at different depths, the deeper reading is shown with a note.
- Issue and pull-request numbers belong to the named upstream project.
- Issue counts in the surveys are keyword hits, not defect counts. They are not used as defect rates anywhere.
- The NeoGraph audit is one self-reported audit, so the defect classes it yields are observations and not incidence rates.

Citations carry no URLs. Each has title, year, and the arXiv id or DOI where the source reports gave one.

## 1. Reasoning state and security

## 6. Architecture

| Citation | Level | What it shows | Decision informed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parnas, *On the criteria to be used in decomposing systems into modules*, 1972, DOI 10.1145/361598.361623 | `[read abstract]` | The decomposition criterion changes flexibility and comprehensibility. ## 9. Live observation (single-sample, dated)

One observation set from the project owner's own API keys, September 2026 (exact dates and sample sizes were not recorded in the source reports, so these are anecdotes and not rates):

- Signed thinking blocks issued through OpenRouter and replayed to Anthropic direct failed with an invalid-signature error in about 40 to 50 percent of attempts. The cause is unknown (routing to different backends, account effects, or something else were not separated).
- OpenRouter returned HTTP 200 even when the replayed signature had been tampered with, so its acceptances are not evidence that a signature was checked.
- On Anthropic, a same-turn tool loop returned 200 after the thinking block was stripped, which shows that acceptance alone does not prove that thinking was replayed.

Evidence level: observed by the owner, not independently reproduced, one account age class, no negative-control run log kept. These observations motivated the canary negative control and N-run rates (conformance plan section 9.1) and the rule that OpenRouter is never in an equivalence class. They do not show that any vendor is faulty.

## 10. Known unknowns

The original reports found no answer to these at their survey checkpoint; the list preserves those research gaps, not today's pending implementation status. Later bounded transport/native/cutover observations are recorded in POC_PLAN and D1, without establishing broad platform support, native consumption or universal vendor qualification. "Decided" remains distinct from empirically qualified.

1. **Reasoning binding.** The Anthropic documentation answers part of it (section 8). Still unverified: behaviour through Bedrock and Vertex directly (no credentials, never tested), through OpenRouter (section 9, cause unknown), and for other vendors' opaque state. The origin design (D4) is exact-origin plus recorded binding facts with an equivalence class that stays `Documented-Unverified` until the canary admits a cell.
2. **Transport backend.** D1 was revised to libcurl on a private Asio loop. The earlier statement that no measured comparison exists is superseded by a local A/B run and the M1 spike (D1 Evidence, POC_PLAN.md section 5). The recorded claim "libcurl HTTP/2 is about 25% slower at p50" was not reproduced: the A/B showed libcurl within about 4% of the Asio pool at 128 concurrent POSTs. A completion-order defect in NeoGraph's libcurl wrapper that adds about 500 ms to both HTTP/1.1 and HTTP/2 is a candidate explanation, but the conditions of the original measurement were not reconstructed, so the cause is not established. Still unknown: HTTP/2 pause behaviour with live sibling streams, the TLS certificate-failure matrix, Windows and macOS, WebSocket (D1b), and behaviour against real vendors.
3. **Async core capacity.** The async-native core is decided (D3), but NeoGraph's real target concurrency is unknown. The benchmark numbers are proposals, and "agent count" is not "in-flight count".
4. **Non-chat features.** They are out of the first release (D2). Whether live users of image, long-running operation and decisions features exist is an owner inventory that has not been provided.
5. **Descriptor rule admission.** No literature gives a cutoff for how many rule kinds a data language can carry. The D5 bar (three independent cases, truth table, permutation test, a prototype with at least 30 percent less duplicated semantic code) is a policy choice, not a natural constant.
6. **Declarative versus code defect rate.** No reviewed work controls for this. The audit has 31 rows from one self-reported audit, and all surveyed projects fixed their reasoning and usage defects in code.
7. **Unknown frame criticality.** Whether a new event is harmless or a new terminal, refusal or usage boundary cannot be decided from taxonomy alone. It needs per-family rules and recorded evidence.
8. **Idempotency per endpoint.** Supported keys, retention, payload-mismatch handling, streaming reconnection and cancellation billing are not established for any vendor. The Idempotency-Key draft was not read.
9. **Usage algebra over time.** Modalities, server-side tools, cache-write TTLs and reasoning inclusion keep evolving. The OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions were only seen second-hand.
10. **Retry budget scope and default.** Whether to share per library instance, process, credential or vendor, and which default suits a desktop client versus a server, is workload-dependent.
11. **Canary sensitivity and cost.** No result gives a minimal probe set that detects all silent vendor changes, nor a false-positive rate. The usage-growth heuristic in the canary is uncalibrated. Vendor terms of service for automated canary traffic were not checked.
12. **Open Responses as an oracle.** Its standing as a client-side oracle is unestablished: it is server-oriented and its schema is reported as OpenAI-shaped.
13. **Migration-diff governance.** Who approves an accepted difference between the current NeoGraph implementation and the new library is a process decision, not a research finding.
14. **Gemini stream ending without a blank line.** Whether any vendor or proxy ends a Gemini SSE stream with a final unterminated event was not observed; the conformance plan carries a synthetic fixture with a decided expectation.
15. **Not covered at all.** swiftide, langchain-rust, TensorZero, Helicone, models.dev, and any vendor interoperability contract for signed reasoning beyond the pages in section 8 were not examined.
